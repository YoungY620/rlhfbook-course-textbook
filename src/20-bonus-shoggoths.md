# Bonus 1: What's With the Shoggoths?

The course page lists one bonus talk with the description: “The meme that explained post-training.”

> **Note:** As of this writing, upstream has not released a full lecture deck, transcript, or recording for Bonus 1 beyond the course-page listing. This chapter is therefore intentionally short and does not speculate about the content. It exists so the bilingual table of contents stays aligned and so readers know the slot is reserved.

The shoggoth meme — an eldritch horror from H. P. Lovecraft, often drawn with a smiling ASCII face pasted on — became a popular way to joke about large language models: beneath the friendly chat interface lies something vast, alien, and not fully understood. In the context of this course, it is a lighthearted entry point into a serious topic: the models we interact with are the product of many post-training stages, and their behavior is shaped as much by data, reward models, and product decisions as by the base weights.

For the substantive treatment of how post-training shapes what a model “feels like” to talk to, see:

- [Lec 8: Preferences and Preference Data](12-lec8-preferences.md) — where the idea of quantifying preference came from.
- [Lec 9: Over-Optimization and RLHF's Bad Reputation](13-lec9-overoptimization.md) — how optimizing proxies produces strange behaviors.
- [Lec 13: Character Training](19-lec13-character.md) — how labs deliberately craft model personality.
