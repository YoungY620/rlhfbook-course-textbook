# Q&A 2: Advanced Derivation Fixes, Resources, and Notation Gotchas

## Math corrections

### Correction: the KD-as-RL-advantage slide (Lecture 7)

A viewer flagged that the on-policy distillation advantage was missing a factor. The reverse KL at state $s\_t$ is an expectation over student-sampled tokens:

$$ D\_{\\mathrm{KL}}\\!\\left(\\pi\_\\theta(\\cdot \\mid s\_t) \\,\\|\\, \\pi\_T(\\cdot \\mid s\_t)\\right) = \\mathbb{E}\_{a\_t \\sim \\pi\_\\theta(\\cdot \\mid s\_t)}\\!\\left[\\log \\pi\_\\theta(a\_t \\mid s\_t) - \\log \\pi\_T(a\_t \\mid s\_t)\\right] $$

Because we sample $a\_t \\sim \\pi\_\\theta$, the single sampled token gives an unbiased estimate $\\hat{D}\_{\\mathrm{KL}}$ of the full KL, and the advantage is its negative:

$$ A\_t^{\\mathrm{OPD}} = -\\hat{D}\_{\\mathrm{KL}} = -\\left(\\log \\pi\_\\theta(a\_t \\mid s\_t) - \\log \\pi\_T(a\_t \\mid s\_t)\\right) = \\log \\pi\_T(a\_t \\mid s\_t) - \\log \\pi\_\\theta(a\_t \\mid s\_t) $$

The old slide wrote a hard “$=$” to the full $D\_{\\mathrm{KL}}$; it is only equal in expectation. Also, the expression $\\log \\pi\_T - \\log \\pi\_\\theta$ *looks* like forward KL, but it is **reverse** KL because the expectation is over the student distribution $\\pi\_\\theta$.

### Correction: KL divergence is not a “distance” (Lecture 0)

KL divergence is **asymmetric** ($D\_{\\mathrm{KL}}(P\\|Q)\\neq D\_{\\mathrm{KL}}(Q\\|P)$) and **violates the triangle inequality**, so it is not a metric. In post-training teams “KL distance” is common colloquial shorthand for “how much has the model changed,” but technically it is a divergence:

$$ D\_{\\mathrm{KL}}(P\\|Q) = H(P,Q) - H(P) \\ge 0 $$

where $H(P)$ is the minimum expected code length for data from $P$ and $H(P,Q)$ is the cost of encoding it with a code built for $Q$. No code beats the one matched to the true $P$, so the KL measures **extra coding cost**, not a symmetric distance.

### Reading: estimating KL in practice

In RL we rarely compute exact KL; we estimate it from sampled tokens. John Schulman’s note on the $k\_1 / k\_2 / k\_3$ estimators is essential lore for anyone touching a KL penalty: [joschu.net/blog/kl-approx.html](http://joschu.net/blog/kl-approx.html).

### Correction: the Bradley-Terry loss derivation (Lecture 2 / Chapter 5)

The per-example derivation of the reward-model loss is clean:

$$
\\begin{aligned}
\\theta^\*
&= \\arg\\max\_\\theta \\; P(y\_c > y\_r \\mid x) \\\\
&= \\arg\\max\_\\theta \\; \\frac{\\exp(r\_\\theta(y\_c \\mid x))}{\\exp(r\_\\theta(y\_c \\mid x)) + \\exp(r\_\\theta(y\_r \\mid x))} \\\\
&= \\arg\\max\_\\theta \\; \\sigma\\!\\left(r\_\\theta(y\_c \\mid x) - r\_\\theta(y\_r \\mid x)\\right) \\\\
&= \\arg\\max\_\\theta \\; \\log \\sigma(\\Delta) \\\\
\\Rightarrow\\quad \\mathcal{L}(\\theta) &= -\\log \\sigma(\\Delta)
\\end{aligned}
$$

with $\\Delta = r\_\\theta(y\_c \\mid x) - r\_\\theta(y\_r \\mid x)$. That last step is only valid **pointwise**. Training is over a dataset, and $\\mathbb{E}[P] \\neq \\mathbb{E}[\\log P]$, so you take the log before averaging:

$$ \\theta^\* = \\arg\\max\_\\theta \\mathbb{E}\_{(x, y\_c, y\_r) \\sim D}\\!\\left[\\log \\sigma(\\Delta)\\right] = \\arg\\min\_\\theta \\mathbb{E}\\!\\left[-\\log\\sigma(\\Delta)\\right] $$

## RL algorithms & notation

### Notation gotchas in Chapter 6

A careful reader caught three notation inconsistencies in the policy-gradient chapter.

**Gotcha 1: reward indexing.** The chapter opens in Sutton & Barto indexing — the reward after $(S\_t, A\_t)$ is $R\_{t+1}$ — but later equations switch to zero-indexed $r\_t$:

$$ R(\\tau) = \\sum\_{t=0}^{T} \\gamma^t R\_{t+1} \\qquad\\longrightarrow\\qquad \\sum\_{t=0}^{\\infty} r\_t \\;,\\;\\; r\_t + \\gamma V(s\_{t+1}) - V(s\_t) $$

The fix is to use lowercase $r\_t$ indexed from zero throughout.

**Gotcha 2: advantage-weighted policy gradient ordering.** The expression

$$ \\nabla\_\\theta \\log \\pi\_\\theta(a\_t \\mid s\_t)\\, A^{\\pi\_\\theta}(s\_t, a\_t) $$

is ambiguous: it reads as if $A$ might depend on $\\theta$. The fix is to move the scalar advantage to the front:

$$ A^{\\pi\_\\theta}(s\_t, a\_t)\\, \\nabla\_\\theta \\log \\pi\_\\theta(a\_t \\mid s\_t) $$

The advantage is a detached scalar; it scales the score-function gradient and is not differentiated.

**Gotcha 3: where do we get RL samples?** The chapter bounced between $\\mathbb{E}\_{\\tau}[\\cdots]$ (under-specified) and $\\mathbb{E}\_{\\tau \\sim \\pi\_\\theta}[\\cdots]$ (imprecise, because $\\pi\_\\theta$ is not a trajectory distribution). The correct subscript is $\\tau \\sim p\_\\theta(\\cdot)$, where

$$ p\_\\theta(\\tau) = d\_0(s\_0)\\prod\_t \\pi\_\\theta(a\_t \\mid s\_t)\\, P(s\_{t+1} \\mid s\_t, a\_t) $$

is the trajectory distribution induced by the initial state, the policy, and the environment dynamics.

### Why switch between $\\nabla\_\\theta J(\\theta)$ and $J(\\theta)$ across algorithms?

REINFORCE is written as a **gradient**:

$$ \\nabla\_\\theta J(\\theta) = \\mathbb{E}\\!\\left[\\,\\nabla\_\\theta \\log \\pi\_\\theta(a \\mid s)\\, A\\,\\right] $$

PPO is written as an **objective**:

$$ J(\\theta) = \\mathbb{E}\_t\\!\\left[\\min\\!\\left(\\rho\_t A\_t,\\ \\mathrm{clip}(\\rho\_t, 1-\\epsilon, 1+\\epsilon)\\, A\_t\\right)\\right] $$

The PPO gradient exists, but $\\min$ and $\\mathrm{clip}$ make it piecewise: it equals $\\rho\_t A\_t \\nabla\_\\theta \\log \\pi\_\\theta(a\_t \\mid s\_t)$ in the active region and goes to zero once $\\rho\_t$ leaves the trust region on the penalized side. Writing it as one expression requires indicator functions for every branch; stating the objective hides that and lets autodiff pick the branch. In code both are a single scalar loss you call `.backward()` on, so the distinction is mostly pedagogical.

## Key Takeaways

- The KD-as-advantage slide is an unbiased estimate of reverse KL, equal to the full KL only in expectation.
- KL divergence is not a metric; it measures extra coding cost and is asymmetric.
- The Bradley-Terry reward loss is maximum likelihood over the dataset, not a per-example probability flip.
- Five chapters provide runnable Suggested Experiments; start with DPO or SFT because they run offline.
- In policy-gradient notation, keep reward indexing consistent, put the advantage outside the gradient, and sample trajectories from $p\_\\theta$, not $\\pi\_\\theta$.
- REINFORCE is a gradient; PPO is an objective. The latter is easier to differentiate via autodiff.
